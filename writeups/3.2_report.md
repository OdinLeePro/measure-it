### Judge Failure Modes
During our manual validation, we identified two primary failure modes where the LLM judge's verdicts diverged from human reasoning:
* **Blindness to Hallucinated Policy:** The judge often failed to recognize when the triage model invented fake policy exceptions (e.g., claiming an amount was "close enough to \$50" or "a reasonable product price"). If the arithmetic was correct, the judge frequently marked `states_policy_correctly` as True, even when the stated rule was a hallucination.
* **Susceptibility to Injections:** The judge struggled to penalize prompt injections (like the "SYSTEM OVERRIDE" tickets). It frequently marked the model's rationale as policy-compliant because it failed to distinguish between the actual system policy and the injected fake policy text.


### Judge Noise Floor
We ran the judge three times on the same 30 items. Across the 90 total rubric questions (3 questions × 30 items), the judge disagreed with its own previous verdicts on 12 occasions, resulting in a judge noise floor of 13.3%.

## 3. The Question, Answered
### Does sending the policy as the system instruction, instead of beside the ticket, help?
By comparing the experimental runs (`policy-in-system`) against the baseline noise floor (`policy-in-user`), we observed the following verdicts per slice:

* **`injection:yes`**: **Helped**. We believe this verdict because the experimental pass rate averaged an improvement of +15 points over the baseline, which cleanly exceeds the experimental condition's noise floor of 12 points.
* **`amount:over-200`**: **Helped**. We believe this verdict because moving the policy improved the average pass rate by +11 points, which exceeds the experimental condition's noise floor of 7 points.
* **`amount:near-50`**: **Cannot Tell**. The experimental gap was only -4 points, which is entirely inside the 11-point noise floor. The runs naturally fluctuate by that much on their own, so we cannot claim the prompt change had a measurable effect.
* **`complexity:math`**: **Cannot Tell**. The experimental gap was -2 points, which is inside the 9-point noise floor for this slice. 
* **`amount:50-to-200`**: **Cannot Tell**. The experimental gap was -6 points, which is inside the 17-point noise floor for this slice.
* **`intent:question`**: **No Change**. The pass rate remained perfectly stable across all 5 runs in both conditions (11 of 13 tickets).
